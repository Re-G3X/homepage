abstract: 'Content orchestration aims to coordinate procedural content generation across multiple facets of game content; however, its applicability across different game genres remains underexplored. This paper investigates the adaptation of a content orchestrator from a top-down adventure game to a 2D platformer, examining its ability to generate engaging and player-adapted content. A mixed-methods evaluation was conducted, combining 168 questionnaire responses from 103 players with qualitative data from think-aloud sessions and interviews with 11 participants. Quantitative results align with prior findings in the top-down domain, indicating perceived balanced difficulty, engaging exploration, and effective lock-and-key mechanics, alongside limitations in rewards, enemy diversity, and quest variety. From thematic analysis, participants described immersive gameplay and positive perceptions of the procedurally adapted content. However, they also complained about game rewards and enemy diversity. The results indicate that the system was able to orchestrate and adapt procedurally generated content across game genres and player profiles. Therefore, this work provides initial evidence that a content orchestrator can be adapted across structurally similar game genres, specifically from a top-down adventure game to a 2D platformer. Our results are a first step towards understanding the conditions under which such adaptation succeeds.'
